# Published evidence on postoperative atrial fibrillation prediction

**Comparison question.** Which published results inform prediction of STS-recorded, treatment-requiring postoperative AF/flutter after isolated adult CABG using structured predictors available by incision? Multicenter hospital-disjoint evaluation is closest to the manuscript's intended use. Related perioperative, monitoring, biomarker, mixed-operation, and clinical-outcome studies belong in the review with their own endpoints and evaluation designs.

## Search and coverage

PubMed searches run on November 25, 2026 returned 102 records on STS/ACSD or CAPS-Care, 428 on CABG prediction, and 107 in the July 2025–November 2026 cardiac-surgery update. There were **566 unique records**. The index retains every hit and its query memberships. Additional discovery used the STS publication index, targeted publisher searches, and reference lists from the systematic reviews. The backward-citation index includes all 39 entries in López Tuesta et al.'s study table, all 19 in Eini et al.'s study table, and 16 additional Eini bibliography candidates. It records sample sizes, validation descriptors, and AUC values as tabulated by the two reviews where available. The comparisons below use primary reports for endpoint and timing. These are discovery counts. We screened all 102 STS-query records for their primary question using titles and indexed abstracts when needed. Forty named POAF evidence in one of the four review categories. The other 62 chiefly addressed related surgical outcomes, pre-existing AF, or other procedures. The tables below prioritize traceable primary results and clinically pertinent STS evidence. Broader reviews cover trials and single-marker work that do not report a usable prediction-model comparator. Embase, Scopus and Web of Science coverage comes from the published systematic reviews. This targeted update is not a new registered, duplicate-screened systematic review. ## What the systematic reviews establish

- Fields et al. found median apparent discrimination **0.71** (range **0.60–0.98**) and median external discrimination **0.61** (**0.51–0.77**). They rated every model analysis at high risk of bias. The paper should cite this distinction instead of claiming a universal pre-incision AUC ceiling. [2025 critical appraisal](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41118128/)
- López Tuesta et al. included 39 studies from four databases searched through July 2025. Six studies performed external validation, 32 of 39 were at high risk of bias, and calibration reporting was inconsistent. [2026 critical appraisal](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42452715/)
- Pandey et al. reported pooled AUCs of **0.60** for the STS score, **0.66** for the POAF score, and **0.68** for CHA₂DS₂-VASc, with a higher estimate for angiographic SYNTAX **0.74**. The STS score here refers to an existing surgical risk score applied to POAF. [2023 risk-score meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37922611/)
- Eini et al. pooled AUC **0.84** (95% CI 0.80–0.87) across 12 quantitatively synthesized studies, with substantial heterogeneity (generalized **I² 87.3%**). Their eligibility includes advanced regression and mixes prediction times and information sets. The pooled value does not define a standard for routine pre-incision STS transport. [2026 CABG model meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijcrp.2026.200619) · [Published methodological comment](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13321023/)
- Farzaneh et al. synthesized **207 CABG studies** and estimated incidence of **24%**. This is incidence and risk-factor evidence spanning different definitions and timing. Separate echo-focused reviews report substantial heterogeneity in atrial strain and volume associations. [Incidence and predictors](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42240420/) · [Echo predictors](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42469664/)
- Pérezgrovas-Olaria et al. synthesized 59 studies and found greater observed POAF incidence with telemetry than with daily ECG in the isolated-CABG subgroup (25% vs. 15%).
