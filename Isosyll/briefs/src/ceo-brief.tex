\begin{minipage}[t]{0.486\textwidth}
  \vspace{0pt}
  \colheading{Always-On Speech Has a Meter That Never Stops}
  {\fontsize{8.45}{10.5}\selectfont
  A recognizer that waits for a wake word or a button press misses what matters. An \emph{always-on} recognizer --- for meeting capture, call-centre assist, in-car and wearable assistants, accessibility captions --- must process \textbf{every second of audio, whether anyone is speaking or not}. That cost never pauses: it is paid in cloud bills, in battery life, and in heat.

  \vspace{2.5pt}
  Today the usual answer is to stream audio to the cloud, where a large model does the work. That adds a network round trip, a per-minute bill, and a privacy question every customer and regulator will ask: \textbf{where does the recording go?} Keeping speech on the device answers it --- if the device can afford to listen.
  }
\end{minipage}\hfill
\begin{minipage}[t]{0.486\textwidth}
  \vspace{0pt}
  \colheading{What Isosyll Changes: Listening Becomes Cheap}
  {\fontsize{8.45}{10.5}\selectfont
  \textbf{Isosyll} is a compact \emph{streaming} recognizer: it writes words while the person is still talking, then quietly corrects the sentence when they pause. One live stream runs at \textbf{\ColdRcTwoLiveLmSpeed\x{} real time} --- an hour of speech in under two minutes --- using \ColdRcTwoLiveLmCorePct\% of one CPU core.

  \vspace{2.5pt}
  Against Zipformer INT8, the fastest open streaming recognizer we measured, Isosyll delivers \textbf{\VsSpeedXOne\x{} the single-core throughput at \VsEnergyPctOne\% of the energy}. Put simply: \textbf{the same core can transcribe \VsSpeedXOne\x{} as much audio, for under 60\% of the energy}. Its end-of-sentence text (\ColdRcTwoFinalWer\% errors) is statistically level with Zipformer's (\ColdZipRsixWer\%); that final pass, unlike live mode, costs more energy than Zipformer (\VsEnergyPctTwo\%).
  }
\end{minipage}

\begin{minipage}[t]{0.495\textwidth}
  \vspace{0pt}
  \begin{govbox}{What This Is Not --- Honest Limits}
    \textbf{Release \releasever{} is strong evidence, not a guarantee. Six limits to keep in view:}
    \begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.10em,itemsep=1.6pt,topsep=2pt]
      \item \textbf{Exploratory, released by decision:} The v3.1.0 model inside \releasever{} \textbf{missed its pre-registered accuracy gates} and was released by owner decision; all results are exploratory.
      \item \textbf{One benchmark, read English:} All accuracy is LibriSpeech development data (audiobooks). No test-set results are claimed; conversational, accented or noisy speech may rank differently.
      \item \textbf{Live text trails slightly:} On the half of the data not used to pick settings, live Isosyll makes \CandLiveLmCDelta{} points more word errors than Zipformer (bounds \CandLiveLmCLo{} to \CandLiveLmCHi). The final text closes the gap.
      \item \textbf{Energy edge is live mode:} the end-of-sentence pass uses \VsEnergyPctTwo\% of Zipformer's CPU-core energy and \ColdRcTwoFinalRss\,MB of memory.
      \item \textbf{Bigger models are more accurate:} 0.6-billion-parameter models (Nemotron, Parakeet) make fewer errors, at far higher compute.
      \item \textbf{Energy scope:} CPU-core energy only --- not wall or battery power --- and no response-time (latency) claim is made here.
    \end{itemize}
  \end{govbox}
